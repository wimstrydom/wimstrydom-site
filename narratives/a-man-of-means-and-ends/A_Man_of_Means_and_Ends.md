**A Man of Means and Ends**

## One

Christian raised his right hand to block the annoying orange glare coming from across the dashboard. He was in combat with the highway traffic. Ahead of him, a slow-moving tanker was impeding his progress. Distorted reflections of the surrounding landscape continuously expanded outwards from the polished steel of the tank’s rear. His left hand clutched the steering wheel; his focus firmly set on the oncoming Toyota. The predictable sound from its approaching tyres reached a peak and dropped sharply as it passed, accompanied by a pulse of air gently nudging Christian’s car.

There! Open road for at least 500 metres, at this speed it was enough. Christian flicked the indicator stalk upwards and floored the accelerator pedal. The transmission dropped a gear, and fuel burned inside the combustion chambers. The revs climbed and he was pushed back into his seat. A controlled movement of the steering wheel angled the car into the oncoming lane as he was propelled forwards and alongside the truck. His eyes flicked between the road and speedometer. One-ten. One-twenty. One-thirty. He was two car lengths ahead. This was an appropriately large margin for safety. He transitioned back into his original lane. These trucks were a constant battle.

The road ahead was smooth, broad and good for overtaking. The contrast of the brilliant white centreline and the black tar indicated recent maintenance. Christian wondered why there were so many trucks here to begin with. These small agricultural towns had industries and retail sectors too, he supposed. He could have probably foreseen this and taken a more optimal route. Committed now though, the only way through this moment was forward. Outside, granite mountain faces dramatically flanked the road on either side, dark grey peaks stretching up towards the sky.

His focus shifted to his wristwatch. On track, only just. The transparent sapphire crystal and brilliant steel glinted in the early morning light. He could see straight into the mechanism through its open heart, and he saw himself in the balance wheel, relentlessly oscillating and propelling the timepiece forward. He was a machine, a force of nature, at this very moment. Ruthlessly attacking the road and speeding towards his destination. Tenacious, single-minded, and flawlessly moving onwards. Nothing could have stopped him now.

His watch was a Zenith, a respectable piece from a well-known Swiss house. He bought it two years ago, using a third of his annual bonus. It had served him well, but looking at it now, he couldn’t help but consider a need to move up towards something from one of the more premier brands. The current one was recognisable, even by some laymen, but in his latest professional and social circles a more refined, elegant, and quietly elevated piece would better sculpt his image. It wasn’t that he was obsessed with optics, in fact he thought of himself as a pragmatist with these things. The watch was merely an aesthetic instrument, thoughtfully wielded as part of his arsenal to progress him upwards in life. He committed mentally to timeboxing an hour later that weekend to find a watch that would make sense for him to target next.

The dark valley collapsed out onto an expansive vista of green plains inlaid with a mosaic of vineyards and fruit orchards. He was fully locked into his driving now, overtaking trucks and other slower drivers in his unrelenting progress. The hypnotising drone of the tyres against the road and the wind rushing over the cabin formed an aural backdrop to the podcast playing on the sound system, occasionally punctuated by brief roars from the engine as he conquered another vehicular obstacle in his path.

He was using the podcast to educate himself on the latest activity in the financial markets. There was a lot of talk about an AI bubble, but from all the latest indications given by the share price movements and analyst recommendations the upward trajectory was not slowing anytime soon.

The host continued, “The thing about a bubble is that it can only really be proven to exist after it’s popped! Yes, the smart money is on riding this wave. But! Also ensuring your portfolio stays diversified. Take the wins along the way and rebalance into your other asset classes as you go.”

“That’s spot on,” the co-host joined in, “you’re never going to time the market, if you risk everything to exit at the top of some supposed bubble, you’re probably going to lose. But if you keep taking profits and shifting them into bonds, real estate, or heck, classic sculptures and precious metals, whatever your allocation is, then you’re guaranteed to benefit from the astronomical growth we’re seeing now.”

While overtaking a string of three cars, Christian contemplated his current portfolio and considered if his asset allocation was appropriate, given his limited risk appetite.

He found himself in a trance, continuously estimating the optimal speed at which to approach his targets, cross referencing the velocity of the oncoming traffic and the bends in the road to minimise braking and ensure maintained momentum. He was speeding past slower vehicles in smooth arcing trajectories, dipping in and out of the oncoming lane with precision to balance speed and safety.

His automotive knowledge base was something he took great pride in. He had inherited the interest from his father. They used to play a game on family road trips, competing to be first in identifying the make and model of each passing car. They had kept score, but the game never finally concluded and usually no winner was ever proclaimed. It had just been a meaningless way of passing the time. It became such a habit though that Christian still unconsciously played the game in his head. Blue Ford Ranger. A pair of VWs, white Polo and silver Golf. Grey Volvo. Unintentionally his thoughts shifted to his father; he was gone now.

“Ah shit!” he muttered as he noticed it was almost eight AM. The sun had pulled a significant gap above the horizon, and golden hour was now gone. Sunrise was at seven-twenty-four, he had looked it up and made a point of remembering. The night before, Tayla had told him over a glass of wine that the valley he would be driving through would light up spectacularly at sunrise. His departure had been timed so that he would transit the valley just then, but he had sped straight through and missed the sunrise, distracted by his onslaught on the glacial trucks. Christian had wanted to stop and get a good selfie to send to Tayla. It would’ve been a thoughtful gesture and could have raised his odds for a third date. He chided himself for missing it. He’d have to find another opportunity to send her something.

## Two

An hour later, the low fuel chime sounded from the car’s instrument cluster. Christian had known this was coming since setting off that morning, but he’d wanted to get out of the city as early as possible. He had figured a refuelling stop in the rural winelands would cost him less time than in the city, where the morning rush hour traffic had been swelling with every passing minute. Still, his car reminding him of his own poor planning frustrated him. It would have been easy to spend thirty minutes the night before to fill up. Now, he would be losing precious time. At least the petrol-stop he’d preprogrammed into his route was coming up as scheduled.

Christian eased onto the brakes and turned off the road towards the quiet station. He couldn’t see any other cars at the pumps, only an old classic parked alongside the convenience store. Perfect. He parked his car next to the final pump, nearest the exit. Gear selector into park, engine off, handbrake engaged. His fingers tapped on the steering wheel. He couldn’t see an attendant. He looked at his watch. The second hand swept across ten markings. This was wasting time. Where is this guy? There seemed to be some activity inside, so it was definitely open. Still no attendant. He undid his seatbelt, opened the door and got out, marching intently towards the dilapidated storefront on the other side of the station. He might as well get a bottle of water since he needed to set things in motion manually, apparently.

As he neared, his eyes were drawn to the parked car next to the shop. He didn’t instantly recognise it, but it looked incredibly familiar. Elegant curves, typical of an early fifties, maybe late forties, American car. No recognisable badges anywhere. He adjusted his path to walk around the front of the vehicle on his way into the shop, hoping to glean some other clue as to its make. Still nothing. It must have been restored recently, the cream-coloured paintjob was immaculate and sharply reflected the car’s surroundings. The bodywork was accented by brilliant chrome details, all spotless. Where had he seen a car like this before?

A fuel attendant finally appeared, ambling around the corner and dressed in a dark blue and red uniform blotted with various oil stains. “Yes boss?”

“Hi! Yes, can you please fill up with petrol? Automatic stop is fine.” Christian gestured to his car.

“Shahp,” the attendant nodded and walked towards his car. 

Christian’s attention drifted back to the unidentified gem. There was no badge on the bonnet, instead just a conspicuously blank spot. It felt strange looking at this John Doe car. No identity. Who would drive such a car? And what use would they get out of it not having an identity? Was it a mere tool? He felt slightly melancholic. He reflected on this feeling, trying but failing to form a full opinion on the lack of moniker.

“Quite something, isn’t she?” an old timer said from behind Christian. 

“Oh yeah!” Christian looked up at the man. He was smiling, watching Christian admire his car. He must have just exited the store, brown paper bag in hand. The man had joyful eyes with white hair peeking out from underneath a checkered flat cap.

“I’m wracking my brain and can’t figure out what this is,” Christian said looking back at the car, moving his head slightly closer, “40s or 50s, American for sure.” He was captivated by this man and his car. He suspected he’d seen this exact car somewhere before.

“She’s a Chevrolet. 1953 Bel Air. Been in the family since my grandfather bought her brand new in the 50s. Your ride’s not too bad either,” said the gentleman, using a glance and an eyebrow to point out Christian’s silver Porsche as it was being filled by the attendant.

“Oh yeah, that’s a lot of fun,” he said, smiling, “but nowhere near as much history as this thing. Why no Chevy badge?” Christian pointed at the blank space on the bonnet.

“Now that’s a funny story. You see, these cars came to the country in kits and were assembled locally. Back in those days, they were badged as Pontiacs in the export market though.” The conversation reminded Christian of the stories his dad used to tell him. The old man continued, “Gramps just hated that. He was dead set on owning a Chevy, and so when he ordered this one, he got in touch with one of his mates at the assembly plant and requested that they leave it unbadged.”

Christian was fascinated by the car’s rich history. How many stories did this car have to tell? “And so, he preferred to drive a blank car?” he inquired, keen on hearing more but getting restless. He had to get back on the road. 

The man chuckled, “Well, he always planned on getting a real Chevy badge mounted, but just never got around to it. In the end it just stayed that way. We always joked in the family that it was a knock-”

“Cash or card, sir?” The attendant was back.

“Card please!” he said to the attendant, who nodded and started walking back to the pump to collect the terminal. 

Christian turned back to the Chevy’s owner, “Sorry! I’ve got to get going. Enjoy the drive! It’s a beautiful day for it!”

“Oh, thank you, likewise. Don’t go killing yourself in that thing!” he said with a pleasant look on his face. Christian could see the man was a bit sad the conversation had been cut short, but with every passing minute all the trucks he passed were getting closer. He had to keep moving. He paid, got in his car, foot on brake, started the engine, and heard it come to life.  Moving off, he glanced back at the man. He was waving. Christian waved back.

## Three

He rapidly got back up to his target pace, a careful 9% above the posted speed limit to avoid speeding fines. Now he was making progress. The plains outside gave way to gently rolling hills and soon he was barrelling straight towards another mountain range. A small twisting road wormed its way along a central ravine. Frustrated, he reduced his speed as he came upon a train of slow-moving cars. More traffic, and no chance of passing.

The boredom of moving slowly brought his thoughts to his destination. He didn’t feel particularly intense emotions surrounding the weekend. He briefly considered this and supposed it was one of those things that just happen in life as you get older. It would be good to see everyone. His sister was already there. She had made most of the arrangements, including the catering. He had his responsibilities too. Mostly just to attend, but there were some things he had to make sure of, even if just for himself.

Contorted, folded rock strata towered above the road, filled with cracks running in all directions. The ancient formations of bright orange stone were accented with contrasting green shrubbery abundantly populating the rockfaces. No doubt this spectacle contributed to the absurdly slow pace he was currently being subjected to. Everyone distracted, gawking around, wasting time. Did they not have places they wanted to be? He considered a gap, but decided it wasn’t worth the risk. He groaned, clutching the steering wheel with both hands. The scenery continued, unabated.

Eventually he cleared the mountain pass, finding himself driving through the little town at the opposite end. More slow-going. The town’s main road was quiet, with various restaurants, cafes, and museums lining the sidewalks. Could be a good place to recommend to friends who were looking for quaint rural towns to visit. He mentally filed away the fact for later use. He finally reached the edge of the town and got back into his rhythm, seeing at long last open road in front of him. Hopefully smooth sailing from here.

Another hour of driving passed, the mountain range remaining visible off to his right. The landscape dried out, and green orchards slowly gave way to the warmer tones of an arid environment. Without any immediate challenge to overcome Christian started playing out how he thought that evening would go. What different people’s expectations might be and what he could do to mentally prepare. He supposed it might not be the best to arrive at his destination on an empty stomach. Who knows how much time he’ll have to eat anything. He tapped at the car’s screen and searched for drive-thrus or fast-food restaurants in the town coming up, the last civilisation before the final stretch. No results. Not surprising out here in the sticks. The best he could find, glancing at the screen, was a diner right on the main road he’d be driving along. That’ll have to do. He hit the phone icon and two rings later someone picked up. He gave his name and mentioned he would be there in about twenty minutes. He enquired about the quickest items on the menu and ordered a roast chicken sandwich.

He rounded a hill and the town came into view, spread out across the side of a mountain, spilling out into a dry valley below. He spotted the diner, decently popular, judging from the number of parked cars. It didn’t look overly busy, however. Hopefully it would be operating in the sweet spot of efficiency. Enough people to have the kitchen working at maximum effectiveness, but not so many that there’d be a queue. He slowed into the parking lot, checked the time and assessed that he had around fifteen minutes to spare before falling behind schedule.

The diner was adjacent to the main road, nestled amongst a few trees that likely predated it. The building was surrounded by a low wooden post fence of the type you might tie a horse to. Wrapping around the building was a deck where some diners sat in conversation. Steel beams supported a rusty corrugated roof, gentle sloped curves forming the top of the wrap-around veranda. Several chrome chimneys of varying heights rose above the surface of the roof. Exiting his car, Christian was met with a wall of heat. The non-particular smell of cooking food dominated the air as he approached. He marched in through the front door and stood a few paces beyond the door’s threshold. Scanning the room, he caught the eye of the hostess and silently announced his presence with a lift of the chin.

“I’ll be with you now, my love!” she called out loudly, busy gathering a set of menus.

The walls, pillars, and ceiling were decked out with old road signs, car badges, and hub caps. Restless, his eyes wandered, admiring the old signage and nostalgic memorabilia dotted around the room. Many of them must have been imported, obviously not being of the local standard. 

The hostess walked over, “Afternoon! Table for one?”

“Hi, I phoned ahead. Christian. I ordered a chicken sandwich.”

“Oh, you’re Christian!” She smiled, “Yes, your food should be ready soon. Let me get your table ready.” She left before he could protest to ask for a takeaway.

“Excuse me, Christian? Are you Stephen Owen’s son by any chance?” a woman’s voice asked from behind him.

He turned to face her, an elegant woman with shoulder length blonde hair and an attractive face. Probably in her fifties, maybe about twenty to thirty years older than Christian. He didn’t recognise her. “Yes, uhm…”

“Sorry!” She smiled, eyes squinting slightly, giving a quick shake of her head. “You probably have no idea who I am. This must be so strange. I knew your father, and the resemblance is striking! I’m Michelle.” She extended a hand.

Christian was caught off guard but intrigued. Her sparkling blue eyes met his. He smiled and shook her hand, “And I’m Christian, but you knew that already. How did you know my dad?”

“Oh, we dated for a few years.” She smiled as if she had said something funny. She had a fine nose and a broad smile. “A lifetime ago now. We briefly met up again last year, before the end. He mentioned you.”

Michelle put a gentle hand on his arm, sincere eyes flicking away and back to his. “I was so sad to hear of his passing.” 

“Thank you, I-” he hesitated, unsure of what to say. He’d never heard of, much less met, anyone his father had dated before his mother.

“I’m so sorry, this must be a lot for you!” she pulled back slightly. “I thought I was going mad when you marched in through the door, and I just had to know. You must think I’m some kind of crazy lady.” She had a crisp, youthful laugh.

“Oh, that’s okay. I won’t lie; this is certainly a lot more than the chicken sandwich I was expecting to find here.” His schedule was still lingering at the back of his mind, but he had to know more about this woman and her relationship with his father.

The hostess appeared next to them, “Sir, I have your table ready if you’ll follow me.”

Christian hesitated, eyes glancing back at Michelle. Before he could say anything, she jumped in, “Do you want to have lunch together? Feel free to say no.”

“I’d love to.” He looked at the hostess, “Sorry can we make it a-”

“Table for two?” the hostess finished. “Yes, that’s fine, follow me, I’ll just get you a menu.”

They followed her through the maze of tables and chairs to an outside bench on the deck. The table was in the dappled shade of a tree, filtering the summer afternoon sun and spreading a mottled pattern of light and dark across Michelle’s white linen dress. A welcome breeze came up from the valley, counteracting the heat and occasionally transporting a faint floral aroma of arid foliage up to them.

“I must admit, I can’t stay for very long, I am on a tight schedule right now, and don’t want to arrive too late.” Christian said, hoping to manage expectations.

She smiled, “That’s okay. When we were on road trips together Stephen would always refuse to stop unless it was absolutely necessary.”

“So, when did you meet my father? He never told me about his dating life before my mother.” He smiled at his own bluntness.

“Oh, I was twenty-two, and he was… twenty-nine, I think? He had just quit his job, and we met at a party of a mutual friend.” She was lost in the memory, eyes looking out over the railing at nothing in particular. “I initially found him so annoying. He had big plans and no one that spoke to him could get away until they had heard all about what he had in store for the future. I had just graduated and wasn’t taking life very seriously. I didn’t have much interest in a job or a career at that stage. I loved being spontaneous and just going along with what the universe threw my way. I just wanted to travel around and see the world, which at the time was much harder than it is now.”

Christian was listening, captivated by this portal into his father’s earlier life. They had always been close but had drifted the last few years. His death came so soon it left him feeling like there was so much he never got to find out.

“It wasn’t until we saw each other again a few months later that he asked me out. For whatever reason he was a lot more charming that time around, and so I decided ‘What the hell, why not?’” She clearly enjoyed talking about this time in her life.

“How long were you seeing each other?” Christian asked. It did feel slightly strange to be hearing about these details from his father’s life, but his curiosity wasn’t easily stalled. It felt like he had discovered a proxy for having the missed conversations with his father.

“Oh, I think it was about three years. On and off, throughout that period. His big plans, and my free-spirit made things tricky.” She smiled at the thought. “We had so much fun. He had the tendency to forget all about his big stuff going on when we were together. At least for a while. Eventually, they would always start rearing their head again though and he’d get restless.”

They sat in silence for a few heartbeats, enjoying the afternoon breeze and listening to the din of conversation layered with the noise of occasionally passing cars.

“How did it end?” he asked.

“Well, ultimately, his grand plans meant that he needed to move away, and I didn’t want to tag along, I enjoyed my life too much. I also told him there was no way I was doing long distance, and so that was that.” Her eyes turned melancholic.

A few minutes later his sandwich arrived, she ordered a summer salad, and they continued talking for another half hour. She told him about the man she fell in love with and what he used to say about how he wanted to raise his children. He told her about their experiences together, the road trips, family vacations, and about what it was like growing up with him as a father, highs and lows. They shared laughs about his most distinctive mannerisms, and together they figured out that she was there when he adopted one of his classic mantras, often repeated throughout Christian’s childhood.

In these moments he felt longing, sadness, but most of all joy. He was constantly aware of time slipping away from him. Every few minutes a truck would rumble through. Initially he barely noticed, but one by one they were wearing him down, and he found himself distracted.

“Have you got anyone special in your life right now?” Michelle asked him, eyes inquisitively looking into his.

“Not really. Romance for me has been mostly a way of passing the time. I guess I just haven’t come across someone I’m passionate enough about.” 

“What does ‘not really’ mean?” she probed, playfully narrowing her eyes at him.

Christian chuckled, “There’s a girl I recently met, Tayla. She’s lovely, and there’s an excitement to it right now, but I’m not head over heels or anything like that.” He shared more liberally than he usually would in response to these questions. Partly, because he felt his guard lowered by Michelle and her charming manner, partly because he knew it would be unlikely that they would cross paths again unless he sought her out.

“Oh, it’s not easy. But it’s also not that hard, if you’ve got an open heart and you’re not too preoccupied by the ‘why’ of it all. That could also just be me, an old romantic that loves first and asks questions later.” She tittered at herself.

“Anything more for you guys?” the waiter enquired, clearing their plates.

This gave Christian the nudge he needed. He checked his watch.

“Do you want to get a peanut butter milkshake with me? Stephen and I always used to share them. I know you’re on a schedule, but don’t you think that would just be the perfect end to this lovely afternoon?” Michelle suggested, restrained enthusiasm shining through. Christian had many memories of his father making peanut butter milkshakes on hot days while they were on holiday at the dam. She was right, it would be perfect right now, but it seemed a waste of time and would only delay him further.

“Oh, that would be lovely but, I’m sorry, I need to keep moving.” He turned his attention to the waiter. “Can I pay in front, on my way out?”

The waiter nodded and went off to prepare the bill. Christian and Michelle exchanged numbers, promising to keep in touch, though he suspected it would all be for nought. After saying goodbye, he turned and walked back inside. The warm glow of the sun was replaced by the cool air conditioning of the diner. He paid his bill, bought a bottle of water, and got back on the road just in time to avoid getting stuck behind a lumbering construction truck crawling its way up the hill.

## Four

He escaped the town at speed, rose above the hill and started the descent. Ahead of him lay a flat expanse of dusty plains, little hills and orange rock formations scattered across its surface. The road stretched, unbending, to the horizon, seemingly infinite. Here he could really move, and his car was being put to good use, propelling him ever closer to his destination. His podcast eventually concluded, and he felt sufficiently informed on the latest stock market news around the AI bubble that may or may not exist.

Sitting in silence, with only the white noise of his own velocity for company, he found he was internally replaying the conversation with Michelle. He had discovered so much he never knew. The conversation was enjoyable, but indulgent, and now he was paying the price. Such poor discipline to let time run away from him like that. It was not planned and it wasn’t productive at all. The reason he was on this trip was to get to his destination. He had to stop getting distracted so often.

He wondered how the preparations were going. It wasn’t going to be a big affair. Only around twelve people, closest family and friends. It would be good to get there soon and then finally relax. At his current rate it would only be another hour. As he drew nearer, his mind became occupied by his father. Soon Christian would be the same age his dad was when he was born. How much progress had he made in life up until now? Had he achieved what he ought to have?

The broad road, with its easy overtaking, became narrower and the population of other travellers dwindled. He was alone on the road, free to pursue his destination at speed. This was the middle of nowhere. The turnoff to the final stretch was ahead. He slowed the car and glided off the paved road onto the gravel track, brown dust and loose rocks organised roughly into the shape of a road. The hum from the paved road was replaced by a rattle, vibrations echoing through the whole car. The conditions were worse than he remembered. As he progressed, the wheels were kicking up dust dramatically, creating a hazy cloud of orange sand behind him. He couldn’t go too fast here, not with the Porsche. Dips and holes in the road kept sneaking up on him and the suspension was being put to its limits. “Fuck!” He hit a particularly large dip. That was a big one. He should slow down, as much as it pained him.

One kilometre later a high-pitched beep came from his dash, piercing through the drone of the road. He glanced at the screen. Tyre pressure warning. What the fuck? Not now! Please! He stared at it in disbelief for a few beats before slowing to a stop and pulling off. The only way through was forward. The car stood half in a ditch, half amongst the teal-grey shrubs and bushes that dotted the landscape here.

Christian got out of the car and immediately felt the sun’s heat bearing down on him. The almost perfect silence was broken by the continuous buzz of insects and the faint metal tings of the mechanical components cooling down, brought on by the uneven contraction in the steel. He inspected the two tyres on the right and saw no faults. Maybe it was just the loose gravel throwing off the computer? He walked around the car to check the other tyres. Rear seemed fine. Front…

“Ah shit!” Yeah, that’s flat. He’d have to get the repair kit and compressor. This would cost him at least twenty minutes. He got back in the car and turned the steering to the right until it was at full lock. The heat was oppressive and he could feel sweat trickling down his back. He took a bottle of water in hand and opened the front of the car to get the compressor out. Where is this fucking hole? He hooked up the compressor and crouched down next to the wheel. He pressurised the tyre and started pouring water out onto the rubber’s surface, looking for the little bubbles indicating a leak. He’d barely gotten started when he saw it.

“Oh, for fuck’s sake!” he roared, straining his voice and forcing all the air out of his lungs, certain that no one could hear him. There was a centimetre long gash in the sidewall. Unrepairable. No spare, and no civilisation for an hour’s drive in any direction. Christian was fuming at himself. How could he be so stupid? Driving a sportscar into the desert. Moronic! A pure self-indulgent decision made only because he wanted to treat himself. An idiot could’ve told him that he should take something more robust given his destination. 

The flash of anger quickly passed, and he was left standing in the sun holding a half-empty bottle of water. He looked at the deflated tyre and saw himself. No, he refused to lose. He used a splash of water to rinse his hands of the dirt and black rubber streaks, then drank what little remained. He got back in the car. It was blisteringly hot. Christian hadn’t even noticed up until now; his shirt was close to being soaked. He pulled out his phone.

“Oh, thank God!” He had one bar of signal.

He rang his insurance’s emergency roadside assistance and explained the situation, providing them with the GPS coordinates. They would be in touch momentarily with the timeline for help to reach him. Resigned, he tossed his phone onto the passenger seat. All he could do now was wait.

Sitting in the shade of his car with one door open, the faintest whisper of a breeze was welcome in this heat. He closed the door and started the engine just to get the air conditioning going. It felt like heaven. The relief from the overwhelming heat was a near emotional experience.

The house was still another forty-five minutes’ drive away. He should let his sister know of his delay. Christian took up his phone again and picked out her contact, hitting the phone icon. Five rings later, she picked up.

“Christian!” she answered with a cheerful tone. “How’s the drive going? Are you through the pass yet?”

“Hey sis, yeah, I’m fairly close actually. Thing is, I got a flat in the Porsche and don’t have a spare, so I’m a bit delayed. Sorry about that.”

“Oh no! That’s terrible. Where are you?”

“I’m on the final stretch, probably about forty-five minutes out. I’ve already phoned roadside assistance, and they should hopefully be here soon. I might still make it before sunset.”

“You’re always in such a rush. Did you even stop for lunch?” She had concern in her voice, but he felt slightly judged.

“I did actually! I met a bizarre woman, said she dated dad before I was born. Do you know a Michelle Starling?”

His sister burst into laughter, “What?! No!”

“Yeah, supposedly recognised my resemblance to dad, and apparently he’d told her about me before he died.”

“You’re joking! What did you say?”

“We ended up having lunch together and telling each other stories about him. It was actually really wholesome.” Christian’s mood was rising now. It truly was a strange interaction and sharing the absurdity with his sister brought with it some joy. “She seemed really passionate about him. Did you really not know about her? You were closer to dad by the end.”

“No! I mean, he told me that there was some girl before mom that he’d wanted to marry, but that it wasn’t really meant to be. Of course, we both know there were many after mom. Dad was always so restless.” she said, sobering up from the laughter.

“He was. And it never got him anywhere. He didn’t know what he wanted and made it everyone else’s problem.” he said with resignation in his voice.

“Yeah, I know.” she said gently.

“It just fucked things up for everyone else. I loved him so much. I wanted to be like him so much.” Christian didn’t have the energy to stop the emotion. 

“I’m sorry, brother. I know it’s been really tough.”

“There’s nothing to show for it all now. What’s even the point of trying so hard? Like you build and build and steam ahead, and then what?”

They sat in silence. His face was feeling hot, nose stiff like he’d walked into a wall.

“And then it all just ends! It all just fucking ends! And it means fucking nothing. No point, no reward, just silence.” His voice was emphatic. He ran out of air after every word and had to gasp before starting the next.

“Christian.” His sister gently stopped him, “I think you’re too hard on yourself, and on him.”

“You’re right. I’m sorry.”

“Don’t say sorry.”

“I’m just exhausted. This has been… a really rough day. The road was insane, you’re lucky you came yesterday.” 

“Yeah.”

They sat in silence for a few more seconds.

His sister interrupted the pause, “You know, we’re always here for you?” 

“Yeah, I know. Thank you.” Where was the roadside assistance? “Look, I think I should go. Need to follow up with the roadside assistance, check where they are.”

“Okay. Let me know if we should come get you once you know more.”

“Yeah, will do. Talk later.”

He ended the call and phoned the roadside assistance again. The closest available tow truck was three hours out and they couldn’t bring a tyre. All they could do was move the car to the nearest workshop. They were still trying to find closer options. He was frustrated but resigned to his fate. Only forward. He asked them to pause. Christian tried to come up with a plan, he could ask his sister to come get him, but then his car would be stuck here. He couldn’t think straight.

He got out of the car. He had to stand and breathe, despite the sweltering heat. He bent over and put his hands on his knees and stared at the ground. Four seconds of breathing in. Hold four seconds. Out four seconds. Hold four seconds. Breathe in four seconds.

## Five

Was that Madonna? He looked up from the way he came. An old red pickup truck was rumbling along with a plume of smoke and dust behind, blasting Material Girl with open windows. This thing was older than he was. It was getting larger. The vehicle slowed down and lurched to a stop next to Christian with the stereo’s attack on silence continuing mercilessly.

“Howzit?!” an overly tanned man shouted at him amidst Madonna’s singing. Topless and wearing shorts with an alarmingly bad pair of knock-off Ray-Bans, he was probably around the same age as Christian. “You Christian?” he shouted with a smile. “Not the religion! Your name!” he laughed.

“Yes! Who are you?” Christian had to shout back to be heard over the music.

“I’m Trevor! Your sister phoned. I’m going past your place. Get in, I’ll give you a lift.”

“Will my car be fine here?”

“Oh, for sure! There’s no one around here. It’ll still be here waiting for you in a hundred years if you don’t come get it.” Who the hell was this guy?

“Okay. Let me get my stuff.”

Christian gathered his things and locked his Porsche, reluctantly dropping his leather duffle bag into the back of the dusty pickup truck and getting into the cabin. They jerked forward and slowly picked up speed with great strain and effort from the engine.

Trevor finally turned the music down to only mildly deafening. “So, you’re Lisa’s brother?” It wasn’t a question. “What possessed you to drive a car like that here?” he laughed, with not much indication of wanting an answer. 

“Yeah, I didn’t quite realise how rough the road would be.”

“You’ve not visited before?”

“Long ago, first time in about a decade.” 

“Oh yeah, it’s pretty bad. You also got a bit unlucky though, I think. I’ve seen some crazy cars get through fine on this road.”

“How do you know my sister?”

“I’m on the farm next door. We do the quadbikes!” Christ knows what that even meant. “She phoned saying you’re stuck here with a flat. Can’t believe they don’t give you a spare with that thing. So much money and they can’t even give you a spare. Hah!” 

Madonna finished her song and gave way to Heaven Is a Place on Earth. 

“Oh, I love this song!” the volume dial went right back up to its former position. Christian laughed at the contrast of his current environment with that of an hour ago. Belinda Carlisle’s angelic voice and drums reverberated through the cabin, turbulent wind rushed in from the windows, and the truck rumbled along as the guitar riffs came in with full force.

“Fuck! It’s a nice one today, hey?” Trevor shouted, smiling like he’d just won the lottery. “Your drive must have been epic! Such beautiful mountains on the way here.”

“It was fine, lots of traffic to pass.”

“Hah! You must do what I do! Drive slow enough and you don’t need to pass anyone. Everyone else’s problem then!” he laughed again.

Rattling along the dirt track, the truck felt like a loose collection of parts held together only by the goodwill of the universe; threatening disintegration at every bump and pothole but stubbornly refusing to yield to the kinetic onslaught. Its intentions were unknown. Was it waiting for its moment to fall apart and inflict maximum damage, or loyally holding together for its owner until it had served its purpose and could move on without hurting anyone?

They didn’t speak much for the rest of the drive. Songs from the eighties briefly overlaid with remarks and comments from Trevor about the scenery or the weather. He seemed like a man that would be content and happy in just about any situation if you gave him a cold beer and a booming sound system. Christian didn’t envy him. It seemed a pointless existence.

They turned the corner to the old house. A row of old eucalyptus trees lined the drive. How they thrived here so effortlessly, he had no idea. The house came into view. An enormous construction of white plastered stone in the Dutch colonial style. A green metal roof framed by rounded masonry at each end. Numerous large windows made up of smaller rectangles were arranged in a grid on the façade facing them.

“Bloody palace this place, hey?” Trevor remarked as they came to a stop next to the front door.

“Thanks for the ride, man. Appreciate it,” Christian said as he got out, and picked out his now very dusty bag from the back. 

“Hundreds! Anytime. Your sister’s got my number, just shout if you guys need anything else!” The truck started off and the cacophony faded into the distance.

The green front door of the house opened, and his sister, wearing a yellow summer dress, came rushing out. “Christian!” she hugged him. “So good to see you! Happy Birthday!”

“Thanks, it’s been quite the journey.” He was smiling, but slightly sombre. He was happy to see her, but exhausted.

“You made pretty good time all considered! We still have an hour or so before dark and plenty of time to get things ready for your big party tomorrow.”

They walked into the house. The hallway was cool, with the polished black and white checkered tile floor stretching all the way out to the back of the building. In between wooden door frames leading to the sitting rooms and lounges, various paintings lined the white walls. He could hear water splashing and music coming from the outside deck area ahead. Sunlight emanated from the open door and reflected off the tiles casting a glow into the room.

His sister skipped ahead and smiled at him, “Come, join us at the pool! We’re having margaritaaas!” she drew out the last aah in a jovial way. 

“That sounds great, I’ll join in a bit. I just want to get a handle on the roadside assistance, see how we can sort something out.”

“Okay, but don’t miss the sunset!” she trotted out into the sunlight.

Christian went up to his bedroom and deposited his things. The window was slightly ajar, letting a warm summer breeze roll through, gently disrupting the airy white drapes. He sat on the edge of the bed and phoned the roadside assistance once again. After close to an hour of phoning back and forth, he managed to arrange something he found satisfactory. He headed back down the stairs and stood in the hallway. The glow coming from the backdoor had dimmed. The last light was dying away. He looked at the door and thought of what lay beyond, “Only way through…”
