**The Mouthpiece**

Horns! Blaring under the strain of the players' lungs. Vibrating the air with such force it would feel like the end of the world did it not happen so bloody often. A child was crying. Or maybe that was yet another new instrument added to the procession. Lucius was unsure. He didn't care all that much other than wishing an end to this vain onslaught on his senses. He could swear it was getting longer every time. The doors opened, and dear lord, now there were drums. He was certain there hadn't been drums before. The barely melodic cacophony continued for another few seconds before finally ceasing. The hall was silent, but the crowd's ears would be ringing for a long while after. Perhaps that was the purpose, he wondered. To extend the presence of this outrageous ritual beyond its window of direct performance. To say, "Wherever you go, this oppression reigns over you." 

Oh. He should speak, "Welcome, brothers and sisters, to church!" He proceeded to give an awful sermon. He wasn't even entirely sure if it made any sense. The new doctrine was practically incomprehensible to him. Standing there watching the ritual consecrations being completed, he again found himself questioning the decision to commit to this change. Oh God, the horns were coming again. He gritted his teeth as his eardrums threatened to pop. Enough of this. The hall wasn't empty yet, but he started moving slowly backwards with a sombre expression on his face. Hoping no one would notice or care. No one is paying attention to him anymore, surely. All of them just wanted to get away from this noise too. He made it to the threshold of the gap in the wall behind him. Passing into the hall beyond, he side-stepped out of view. Why did he do that? He felt so silly. At least he spared himself a few minutes of presiding over the aural assault on the congregation.

The thrum reduced in intensity and his footsteps on the cool stone floor became more audible. He reached his chamber and sat down behind the enormous desk, sermon notes and doctrinal books haphazardly sprawled across the surface. Technically this wasn't his chamber anymore, with all the sacred custodianship principles that had been introduced. Nonetheless, no one would presume to occupy it while he was still here. He stared at his notes and the open books of doctrine. That sermon was so hurried, he felt like a child who had ad-libbed an oral exam. He had changed the topic an hour before the service as he became reasonably certain that he must have fundamentally misunderstood one of the prime tenets he was to speak about. Thankfully, he didn't have to worry about that again for another seven days. For now, some cheese was what he needed. One of the servants should be around soon.

Footsteps in the hallway outside the door, growing louder. Though the door itself was long gone, having been taken off its hinges, along with the railings and shutters on the windows. Only gaping holes remained now, exposing him to the outside world. Maybe the footsteps would bring some cheese. They rudely brought Factor Senvien into the room instead. Shit and Geist! He hadn't realised Senvien would be in attendance today. Hopefully, he hadn't listened to the sermon.

"Hello, Senvien! How kind of you to grace my chamber this afternoon!" he said in as sincere and welcoming a tone as possible.

The Factor stood opposite the desk, towering over Lucius and beaming a broad smile at him from his perfectly symmetrical face, "Not your chamber," he said in a singsong tone accompanied by a little finger wag. "Don't make me think you've not yet freed yourself from the tyranny of ownership, Acolyte Lucius."

Such an insufferable and entitled little shit. Being called an Acolyte by someone half his age grated. "Oh, you must pardon an old man his-"

"Speaking of which, I must admit, I found your sermon today to be a bit convoluted." 

That's because your doctrine is convoluted, Lucius thought to himself.

"And not surprising, looking at the state of your preparations," Senvien said while raising a disapproving eyebrow at the clerical detritus on Lucius's desk. "You really need to improve your handwriting, Lucius, this is barely legible."

"I suppose we all have areas we can improve and grow in. In my humble opinion, what's importa-"

"What's important is that you make requisite progress for advancing to Prelate within the next two months! You may have inherited your position and duties here from your former existence, but you still need to advance according to the divine principles. Without this…" 

Senvien, fully entranced by his own lecturing now, turned and moved towards the window, looking out towards the mountains in the distance. Lucius was barely listening anymore, resigning himself to his role as silent schoolchild being chided by the headmaster.

"…and yes, the expansion is aided by the continuity of old clergy, and you made the right decision to stay rather than be exiled, contrary to so many of your former colleagues. They are all now…"

Two years ago, he had never even heard of Senvien. Lucius had been a distinguished leader of this church and was the voice of truth for the entire city, bringing certainty of absolution to everyone. He was respected by his subordinates, the other elders deferred to him in all matters. Now they were mostly gone and reviled him as an apostate from a distance. They could not have done anything for the city from the other side of the ocean! That's what he had told them. The truth was that, at his age, he might not have survived the journey, and his position in the temple was all he had. So many of them had family wealth that would provide for them. He would have become a glorified beggar, living on others' estates, sticking around like an unwanted smell. It was either that or stay in the life he'd become accustomed to. All he had to do was just change the words he spoke to be slightly different. But change had come with difficulty for him. The differences were enormous, and he just couldn't absorb them. Now this child Factor was lecturing him in his own chamber. Stood there proudly staring into the distance while monologuing away, his height blocked the light from falling into the room.

"…removed from your position and sent to the temple college in Kavina for reinstruction, as I'm sure you've no doubt made peace with at this point." Senvien's words shocked Lucius out of his ruminations. No! What? He hadn't been listening.

"Wait, I was promised continuity! You can't seriously be saying I would be sent away?" He thought the worst-case scenario was that he would just stop giving sermons and be allowed to live out his days in peace in the city temple, existing as the strange elderly acolyte and left alone to be tormented by his thoughts. That would be bad enough, but being sent away would be the end. He would likely never return.

"Yes, you were gifted with a second life and given the opportunity to prove yourself a worthy mouthpiece." Senvien briefly turned away from the window and gave him a ridiculous condescending smile that made Lucius want to scream. He turned back and continued his staring contest with the distant cliffs. "But it's been close to a year, and your sermon today was evidence enough that the seed has not taken. You don't have nearly as many ceremonial duties for the city's gentry as before. You're not marrying, committing to stone, or raising up any infants these days. For whatever reason, the city no longer sees you as an authority." 

That much was true. He was seen as either a freshly repainted relic of the past by the more receptive families, or a cowardly turncoat by the traditionalist houses.

"You said I had two months left to advance to Prelate. I can still do it; I still have another chance" Lucius was bargaining, and he hated it. He was standing now, knees complaining from the sudden demands, moving into Senvien's shadow.

"You do." Senvien turned around, silhouetted by the sunlight from the window, and looked him in the eyes. "But my voice carries the most weight for that decision, and to be honest, I don't think you have demonstrated the required attributes. Just yesterday, I heard you had reinstated an incredibly expensive kitchen order for regular deliveries of Vyrillian cheese. Is this truly how you think our sacred custodianship should be spent?"

The cheese? His life is being destroyed because of cheese. Senvien was still talking, looking right at him. He had dedicated his entire life to this place, and now it was all being taken away because of cheese. It's not fair! How could this be happening? No one else had ever been treated like this. He needed to do something! He needed to make Senvien understand his place. He was outraged!

"…is a primary tenet to our way of-"

Lucius took two steps forward, seething, "The cheese is-" Resistance gave way, and he felt the sun on his face, hands stretched out into the air outside the window where Senvien had been standing a moment ago. Oh no. What had he done?

Two screams assaulted him at once from both sides. He looked down at the courtyard. Screeching incomprehensibly, Senvien was awkwardly crawling away.

"Shit!"

Right. They were only on the first floor. This was even worse. Behind him, a servant was standing in the doorway, a shocked expression on his face, letting out small frantic shouts in short, hurried bursts, trembling hands barely holding onto a tray of cheese.

"And Geist!"
